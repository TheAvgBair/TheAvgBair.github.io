Make next season's setup easier.

The best time to remember where the lights, table linens, winter gear, or camping accessories went is the moment you put them away. Save the clue before the season changes.

Months later, “somewhere in the basement” is rarely enough.

Separate the category from the precise location.

Tags help you browse a season. The location tells you where to reach.

A record can use “winter,” “camping,” or “holiday” as a tag while the location names the actual container and shelf. Notes can capture the detail that is easy to lose: which bag holds the extension cords, whether the tree stand is with the tree, or where the replacement clips were tucked.

  • Use a broad seasonal tag so related items share a search term.
  • Name containers consistently in the app and on the physical label.
  • Add a selected photo clue when several bins look alike.
  • Save accessories separately when they live apart from the main item.

A two-minute end-of-season routine.

1

Choose the container

Pack the item and label the bin, bag, shelf, or cabinet in plain language.

2

Save the odd detail

Add the accessory, sub-location, photo, or note most likely to help next time.

3

Search by season

Use item names, locations, tags, notes, or alternate names when setup begins again.

Useful seasonal categories.

Save only what tends to send you looking in more than one place.

  • Holiday decorations, lights, hooks, stands, and serving pieces
  • Winter gloves, traction gear, snow tools, and vehicle accessories
  • Camping cookware, tent pieces, adapters, and repair supplies
  • Garden tools, irrigation parts, seed-starting supplies, and covers
  • Patio cushions, furniture covers, outdoor games, and pool accessories

If a whole category lives together and the label already tells the story, one record may be enough. If an important accessory lives elsewhere, give it its own clue.

The goal is not to catalog a season. It is to prevent next season's first day from becoming a search through every bin.
